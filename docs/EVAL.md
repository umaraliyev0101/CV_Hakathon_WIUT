# Showroom Pulse: evaluation

Generated by `pulse eval`; do not edit by hand. Every number below is computed from cached perception output, the MEVA ground truth and `labels/`, and is reproduced by
`.venv/bin/pulse eval --out docs/EVAL.md` (byte-identical on every run, no timestamps, no wall-clock measurement). A missed target is reported as measured.

## Method

- **Data:** six indoor MEVA clips (school scenes, 5 min each, CC BY 4.0, Corona et al., WACV 2021). The dev/test split is fixed in `labels/split.json` before any tuning: **dev** = G423 03-11, G420 03-11; **test** = G423 03-12, G421 03-13, G419 03-09, G299 03-09. Dev and test are always reported separately; the targets are judged on test.
- **Pipeline under test:** preset `dealership` (5-min CCTV), detector `yolox_s`, pose `rtmpose_s`, 5 fps sampling, 960 px wide, CPU. Perception is read from `runs/cache`; the identity merge (`merge_sim_thr` 0.9, `merge_max_speed` 0.25) is re-run from the cache. The two merge knobs were tuned on the dev clips only (`scripts/tune_merge.py`; full trial log in `PROGRESS.md`): `merge_sim_thr` went from 0.75 to 0.9 (pooled dev entry/exit error 35.3% to 3.6%), `merge_max_speed` is insensitive and kept at 0.25. The selection objective was written down before the first run and revised twice after seeing dev results (the per-term cap of 3 was removed, and an interior-optimum condition was added); a first run also used a wrong count-error formula and was discarded. The optimum (0.9) is the same under every version of the rule. **Test numbers were visible during tuning:** draft full evaluations of all six clips at the then-default 0.75 (including the buggy formula) were run and read before `merge_sim_thr` was changed, and the switch from one-to-one to many-to-one track matching (below) was made after seeing match rates on dev and test clips. The tuner itself never loads the test clips and its objective used dev numbers only; after the freeze only report rendering and caching code changed (no metric or configuration change). With MEVA ground truth this tuning rests on two clips and counts only, so treat the new threshold as a dev-set fit, not a general optimum; at 0.9 the merge joins few tracks and, on test, does not improve IDF1 (see Tracking).
- **Roles:** every person is scored as a customer, because MEVA has no staff labels (role detection is therefore not evaluated here).
- **Ground truth:** MEVA geom tracks of type `person`, sampled at the same source frames as the pipeline (`round(k * src_fps / 5)`), boxes scaled to the 960 px analysis frame, foot = bottom centre. The GT is turned into a Timeline and run through the SAME `zones`, `journey` and `stats` code as our output, so dwell, journeys and occupancy differ only through perception and tracking. Zones are the agent-drawn, unreviewed `labels/zones/<clip>.json`.
- **Person matching:** MEVA tracks are annotated per activity, so a GT "person" is a short snippet (median about 1 s on the first dev clip) of someone we may track for much longer. Each GT track is therefore matched independently to the predicted person with the best overlap (overlap = sum of box IoU over the GT track's sample times / its number of samples, minimum 0.3; several GT tracks may share one predicted person). Dwell and journeys are compared inside the GT track's own time window only: our person's samples in that window go through the same `journey` code as the GT samples. GT tracks shorter than `zone_min` cannot produce a zone visit, so few GT tracks yield dwell pairs; the number of pairs is always shown.
- **Library:** motmetrics 1.4.0, IoU >= 0.5 for a tracking match.

## Entries and exits

Ours = `stats.store` entries/exits (entrance-zone rule, spec 4.5). GT = MEVA `person_enters_scene_through_structure` / `person_exits_scene_through_structure` activities, counted over the same window the rule uses: entries starting at or after `leave_after` (10 s) and exits ending at least `leave_after` s before the end (people already present at the start are excluded by definition, spec 4.5). Count error = sum |ours - GT| / sum GT over entries and exits (entries and exits are separate terms, so an over-count of one never cancels an under-count of the other); the pooled figure sums over clips the same way. Timing error: each GT activity is matched to the nearest unused predicted event within 5 s; the median absolute difference is reported with the number of matched events.

## Zone dwell

Per matched (GT track, our person) pair and zone: dwell = summed length of the visits to the zone inside the GT track's window (`zone_min` 1.5 s, `zone_gap` 2 s hysteresis). Median absolute error in seconds and as a percentage of the GT dwell, over pairs with GT dwell > 0. Pairs where we report dwell in a zone the GT person never visited are counted separately (`ours only`). The Dealership target is a median error of at most 10%; the 2 s target belongs to the Short preset and is shown for reference.

**What this measures:** MEVA tracks are annotated per activity and are short (median about 1.2 s on the first dev clip; many are under `zone_min`), and each GT track is matched many-to-one inside its own time window. These dwell figures therefore measure zone assignment inside short GT snippets, not a person's dwell over a whole visit, and they are invariant to the identity merge by construction (identical across all 42 tuning trials). They cannot detect merge errors.

## Journeys

Accuracy = 1 - normalised edit distance between our zone sequence and the GT sequence, averaged over matched persons. Matched pairs where both sequences are empty (the person visited no zone) carry no information and are skipped.

**What this measures:** the same windowing and many-to-one matching as Zone dwell. The sequences compared are the zones visited inside one short GT activity track, not a person's end-to-end journey through a visit, so the 80% target is judged on a metric that is invariant to identity merging (unchanged across all 42 tuning trials) and says little about whole-visit journeys.

## Limitations

- The zones are agent-drawn from one blurred keyframe per clip and have not been reviewed by a human (`labeller: ai-agent, unreviewed`). Dwell and journey errors include any zone-placement error; entrance zones are generous.
- MEVA annotates only people who take part in an annotated activity. Un-annotated people appear in our output but not in the GT: this lowers MOTA and precision and raises occupancy MAE, so the occupancy and tracking numbers are pessimistic. Conversely, GT tracks are often fragmented into several ids for one person, which makes GT occupancy and person counts optimistic.
- The eval clips are school hallways, lobbies, a stairwell and a gym, not showrooms: no cars, no salespeople, no desks, different crowd behaviour and camera heights. Role detection, unserved interest, assistance and stops are not evaluated at all.
- Entry and exit counts are compared over the window the entry/exit rule uses; people present in the first or last `leave_after` seconds are excluded on both sides. Clips with very few GT enters or exits (e.g. G421 enters, G420 exits) give unstable per-clip percentages; read the pooled figure.
- GT tracks are per activity and short (a few seconds), so the zone-dwell and journey comparison rests on few pairs; GT tracks that nobody in our output overlaps (missed people, crowded scenes such as G299 and G421) are left out of those statistics, so they describe only the people we tracked.
- The entry/exit GT is the set of annotated activities, not a census. Where many real people enter or leave without an annotated activity (G421 has 2 annotated enters but we count 44 entries) the per-clip error is mostly un-annotated truth, not detector error; the pooled test figure is dominated by such clips and says little about showroom accuracy.
- The file-mode real-time factor of the Targets table is measured on a quiet 60 s excerpt; on the full 5-minute clips the crowded scenes (G421 lobby, G299 gym, 320+ tracks) run below real time (see Speed). Real-time file mode is not met on crowded footage with this CPU configuration.
- Only six clips from two cameras-days of one school; the dev set has two clips. Differences of a few points are within noise.
- File-mode and live speed are measured on one machine (see Speed), CPU; CoreML, if shown, is reported separately and was measured once.
- No stock showroom or synthetic clip was available, so there is no showroom-domain accuracy number.
