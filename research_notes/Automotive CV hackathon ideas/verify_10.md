## wild-6 Yield-to-Win: cashback for drivers who stop for pedestrians

### Takeaway
The twist (positive, opt-in, plate-matched cashback for yielding) was not found as a product or pilot in two English searches (one extended), and it continues the elimination class `failure_to_yield` and an Uzcard-partnered event, so novelty and local fit are high. The weak points are the payer (insurers cannot discount the Cabinet-fixed OSAGO tariff and the voluntary-motor pool halved in 2025), a reward economy that only reaches opted-in drivers, reward farming, and a demo that is staged. Verdict: sharpen (bank/loyalty sponsor, A/B pilot, anti-gaming).

### Cited Findings
#### Findings
- The Stockholm "Speed Camera Lottery" the pitch cites exists as a Volkswagen Fun Theory video (YouTube title "The Speed Camera Lottery - The Fun Theory"), but no page fetched this run gave its measured speed reduction or vehicle count, and Wikipedia has no page for it (404) — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iynzHWwJXaA) (title only)
- Search found no programme that pays drivers for yielding to pedestrians; results were AI fining cameras (Virginia law letting localities cite crosswalk and stop-sign violations, Obvio "Stop for Kids") and awareness campaigns — [Yahoo News on Virginia law](https://www.yahoo.com/news/us/articles/virginia-law-allow-ai-cameras-222146637.html) [SNIPPET]
- A second search (EN standard: "drivers rewarded for stopping at zebra crossing yielding pedestrians reward scheme city pilot incentive instead of fines") again found no reward scheme; it surfaced cheap non-reward interventions instead. Westminster's paint-only side-road zebra trial (18 monitored locations, 11 sites with crossings installed, article dated 11 Aug 2026) lifted average driver compliance from around 20% at some locations to 70-80% (up to 90% for some movements); a Greater Manchester report found zebra markings without beacons made drivers give way 65% more often [SNIPPET] — [CIHT](https://www.ciht.org.uk/news/westminster-trial-shows-side-road-zebra-crossings-can-transform-pedestrian-priority/) (re-checked)
- Tashkent already fines non-yielding with AI cameras: 10 crossings, about 1,700 fines in three days (Jul 2025), 30 more junctions planned, vendor not named, confirmed to Spot by the Road Safety Service spokesperson (initial report came from a blogger's Telegram channel) — [Spot, 13 Jul 2025](https://www.spot.uz/ru/2025/07/13/cameras-ai/) (re-checked)

### Inferences
- [CALC] City fee 3,000,000 UZS = about 254 USD per crossing-month; box 500 USD over 24 months is about 21 USD plus 25 USD ops = about 46 USD cost, about 208 USD gross margin per crossing-month before camera, mounting and permits (the idea's USD 500 box excludes an LPR-grade camera and IR illumination, so true capex is higher). Reward pool is separate: 500 UZS x 100 rewarded yields/day x 30 = about 127 USD per crossing-month.
- [CALC] Insurer upside is tiny: all 2025 OSAGO payouts are about 20.8M USD nationwide; a 0.1% reduction is about 21k USD a year across the whole country, versus costs of instrumenting crossings.
- [INFERENCE] A reward only changes behaviour for opted-in drivers who know the crossing is instrumented; deterrence by fines already covers all drivers at the 10 camera crossings, so rewards are redundant where fines exist and unobservable where cameras do not exist. Lottery-style variable rewards (the Stockholm idea the pitch cites; I could not re-verify its numbers this run) are cheaper per behaviour change than fixed cashback, but need a visible prize.
- [INFERENCE] The honest counterfactual is cheap infrastructure: if paint alone moves yielding from about 20% to 70-80% (Westminster), a judge will ask why a city would pay about USD 254 per crossing-month for a reward layer that only touches opted-in drivers. The answer has to be measured behaviour change per dollar from an A/B pilot, which the idea does not plan.
- [INFERENCE] Reward farming is the main attack: a friend stands at the kerb while a registered car loops. Needs per-plate daily cap, pedestrian-track diversity check, plate-ownership verification (the bot registers a plate to a card; nothing here proves the registrant owns the plate).
- [INFERENCE] Night and rain: plate legibility at 30-40 km/h needs an IR illuminator and a shutter fast enough to freeze the plate; the pitch's "missed night plates just pay nothing" is honest for rewards but means the system would pay mainly daytime dry-weather drivers, a biased sample that weakens the "behaviour dataset" moat.
- [INFERENCE] The brief asks for a "web version" judges can open; a Telegram-bot-only demo does not satisfy that, so ship a web page (or Telegram Mini App) with the same flow.

### Gaps
- No Russian, Chinese or Uzbek probe for existing "reward courteous drivers" schemes (I recall Chinese city credit schemes and insurer telematics rewards from memory, unverified); Stockholm lottery figures not re-verified (Wikipedia pages for it returned 404).
- No evidence of any Uzbek bank, insurer or city willing to fund cashback; no legal check on a private edge box filming public crossings or on plate-based opt-in under the personal-data law.
- UA-DETRAC and DoTA licence terms not found for commercial training use: Hugging Face mirrors of UA-DETRAC carry a CC BY 4.0 tag (e.g., `abhineet123/ua_detrac`, 1,117 downloads) though the Albany page states no licence; DoTA's repo is MIT-licensed code, with a 55 GB dataset on Google Drive built from YouTube dashcam videos (dashcam footage, not fixed-crossing cameras) — [DoTA repo](https://github.com/MoonBlvd/Detection-of-Traffic-Anomaly); Hub API search "detrac".

## matrix-12 Dash Decoder: dashboard warning triage in Uzbek and Russian

### Takeaway
Photo-to-meaning-and-severity for dashboard lights is already sold by several app-store apps and partly built into iOS, so the core is commodity; the defensible sliver is a verified fault-to-action table for Chinese-firmware strings plus a workshop booking funnel. Strong local context (122.7k EVs, 99.2% of H1 2026 EV imports from China) but unverified pain (interface language of imports) and a tiny revenue pool. Verdict: sharpen to grey-import Chinese EV owners with a hard safety floor.

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] famous = yes: "photo of warning light, what it means, how serious" is shipped by AutoDiag and others and by iOS; the idea's own "nearest" (Google Lens) understates that. The remaining differentiators are Chinese cluster strings, Uzbek/Russian output, and the booking.
- [CALC] Revenue ceiling: 122.7k EVs x 5% adoption x 1 qualified booking a year x USD 3 = about 18k USD a year; "owners USD 2/month later" has no evidence of willingness to pay when AutoDiag sells credits at USD 0.99.
- [INFERENCE] Liability: an answer "drive" on a high-voltage battery, brake, steering or airbag fault is a safety failure; the product needs a hard-coded "stop" floor and an abstain path, which also caps the "drive/slow/stop probabilities" from a decision model nobody has benchmarked (d1-omni was released five days before this check).
- [INFERENCE] The 300 fault strings from owner manuals is a few hours of LLM-assisted work, so the table is not a moat unless tied to workshop-confirmed outcomes. A plausible hook: BYD Royal Motors is an event sponsor (an official dealer would also have its own brand app, unverified).
- [INFERENCE] Synthetic cluster screenshots in real fonts are a weak test of glare, moire and curved screens; at least 30 real photographed clusters are needed for an honest accuracy claim.

### Gaps
- Only one Russian probe (above), no Uzbek-language probe and no check of Uzbek-language support in AutoDiag; no evidence of how many imported EVs ship with Chinese-only interfaces; BYD Uzbekistan site is a placeholder; no check of OEM brand apps (BYD, Chery) that already include warning explanations.

## wow-8 Dispatch Card: crash-clip triage with CNG flag

### Takeaway
No product that turns a bystander crash clip into a structured, bilingual dispatch card was found; the closest are live-video 911 tools without listed AI analysis (Carbyne, Prepared) and several academic VLM incident-report pipelines. The documented Uzbek CNG blasts happened at refuelling stations, not in crash scenes, cylinders are rarely visible, and the stated price ($0.30 per incident) gives a national ceiling of about USD 2.8k a year. Verdict: sharpen (drop or relocate the CNG claim, change the payer model).

- [INFERENCE] Possible rescue: dead-lamp triage only, on fixed city cameras or one bus route, with a daytime inventory; still competes with IoT lamp controllers that report faults themselves.

## city-2 SunkenLid: open and sunken manhole alerts

### Takeaway
Road-asset vendors already inventory manholes (Xweather RoadAI "Maintenance holes", Vialytics "manholes, drains"), municipal pilots rate manhole condition from vehicle cameras (Costa Mesa 1-5 rating, Cologne phones in cleaning vehicles), and open-source code and small datasets exist, but I found no product that scores lid hazard state (open/missing/sunken) and routes tickets to the owning utility. The headline metric (step height in centimetres from single-image monocular depth) is not credible. Verdict: sharpen to a classification-based hazard triage (open/missing/broken/sunken) with an owner-routing table.

### Cited Findings
#### Findings
- Xweather RoadAI lists "Maintenance holes" and "Storm drains" under street furniture and drainage, plus "Overhanging vegetation" and "Safety hazards" as special objects — [Xweather RoadAI](https://xweather.com/roadai) (re-checked)
- Vialytics "recognizes key assets — signs, manholes, drains, markings", photographs the road every 10 ft, "Used by 1,000+ Communities", can "assign work orders"; no condition-state or owner routing described — [vialytics.com](https://www.vialytics.com/) (re-checked)
- Municipal and academic prior art for ML manhole condition from vehicle cameras: Cologne's waste utility AWB and msg ran a "Smart Fleet" pilot with Android phones mounted behind the windshield of city cleaning vehicles (video plus GPS uploaded to Azure; detections as GPS markers on a portal map for maintenance staff); the model detected road damage and manhole covers, and the page says the hit rate was higher for manhole covers than for road damage (no figures, no dates), with traffic-light faults named as a next use — [msg Group](https://msg.group/en/surplus/awb-cologne-smart-fleet-ai-road-damage) (re-checked); Costa Mesa Sanitary District (California) films streets with a GoPro on a code-enforcement vehicle for two weeks each quarter and a machine-learning model identifies manholes and rates condition 1-5 [SNIPPET, page returned 403] — [Costa Mesa SD](https://cmsdca.net/sewer/ai_manhole_management.php); a 2024 study used YOLOX on car-recorder footage to sort manhole covers into normal, broken and down states [SNIPPET, source not opened]; a mobile-LiDAR study of manhole detection exists — [PMC6566314](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6566314/) [SNIPPET]
- Open source is thin: GitHub topic `manhole-detection` shows 1 repo (Swiss Territorial Data Lab, manholes on street-view images and reprojection); `gh search` shows several low-star YOLO course projects, one "manhole cover defect detection system based on YOLOv8" (2026-09) and Chinese repos advertising a 10,000-image, 5-class defect/status dataset (provenance and licence not verified) — [GitHub topic](https://github.com/topics/manhole-detection) (re-checked); `gh search repos "manhole cover detection"`
- Hugging Face has small manhole sets: `delima87/manhole_covers_dataset` (Apache-2.0, 1K-10K images), `liup93/manhole` (MIT), `badsaarow/manhole-worker-safety` (10K-100K, parquet) — Hub API search "manhole" on 2026-10-10
- Models: RF-DETR-Seg Nano 40.3 mask AP at 3.4 ms (T4), Apache-2.0; DA3 Small/Base/Metric-Large/Mono-Large Apache-2.0 (Large/Giant checkpoints are NC); MoGe-2 weights MIT (60 ms ViT-L on A100); FLUX.2 [klein] 4B Apache-2.0; MapAnything Apache weights exist (`facebook/map-anything-apache`) — [tech_radar.md] [TEAMMATE]
- MoGe-2 abstract and the DA3 abstract and README give no accuracy numbers for metric depth; DA3 README only gives a focal-length conversion (`metric_depth = focal * net_output / 300`) — [arXiv 2507.02546](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.02546); [arXiv 2511.10647](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.10647); [DA3 README](https://github.com/ByteDance-Seed/Depth-Anything-3) (re-checked)
- Uzbek plates are 520 mm wide (usable as a horizontal ruler) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicle_registration_plates_of_Uzbekistan) [TEAMMATE]

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] Physical limit: sunken lids are a 2-10 cm problem; single-image metric depth has relative errors of a few percent (no citation obtained this run), i.e. decimetres at 3-6 m, and a plate-width ruler gives horizontal scale, not vertical step. Two-frame parallax or a multi-view model (MapAnything) can do better; a single-photo "step height in cm" would be an unvalidated number.
- [INFERENCE] The inpainting trick (FLUX.2 klein removes real lids, mask = label) produces synthetic "missing lid" images with a large domain gap (clean fill vs a dark hole with debris and shadow); it can seed training, not prove accuracy.
- [CALC] 20,000 UZS per ticket = about USD 1.7; an owner-routing table and a Telegram message are what citizens already get from complaint portals (not verified for Tashkent), so willingness to pay per ticket is doubtful.
- [INFERENCE] The strongest framing is safety (open and missing lids are lethal) with the buyer being the three or four utilities that own lids; but no Tashkent data on lid theft/open-hole incidents or owners was found.
- [INFERENCE] The demo is easy to try (a judge can photograph a drain on campus), which is a plus.

### Gaps
- Chinese and Russian manhole queries did not run (quota), so Chinese "smart manhole" products and CIS programmes are unchecked; no Uzbek incident statistics; ownership of lids in Tashkent unverified; Mapillary Vistas manhole class not verified (page is JavaScript-only); the 2024 YOLOX study and Costa Mesa details are summary-only; whether Cologne tested missing or open covers is not stated.

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] The idea's data line "Public 75k smoky-vehicle set (arXiv 2207.03708, check licence)" fails the check: there is no public set. The team must collect its own (rent or find smoky diesel trucks at a bridge, mine detections, human-verify, as the paper did), or contact the authors, or fall back to zero-shot SAM 3/VLM prompts with weak precision. State this in the data-source declaration.
- [INFERENCE] Expected accuracy for a student system is below the paper's 0.78 F1; report precision/recall at a fixed threshold on a local held-out set and position the output as inspector triage with human confirmation (every real deployment found, including Hainan and Sri Lanka, keeps a human or an evidence standard in the loop).
- [INFERENCE] Winter is the target season (smog inversion, Nov-Feb) but cold exhaust produces white condensation plumes that look like smoke; the paper does not list water vapour as a case, so add it as a hard-negative class; sunset is early and the pitch's daytime-only grading halves usable hours.
- [INFERENCE] Displacement risk: Hong Kong and Pakistan show that governments run this on their own sensors and Safe City cameras (Tashkent's Safe City has 42,750 cameras per Kun.uz, Jan 2026); a smoke module from an incumbent camera vendor or an integrator is the competitor, so the moat is local data, calibration with inspectors and the repeat-offender ledger, not the detector. The Hong Kong notice-repair-retest loop (96% repaired) is the model to copy: an owner repair order plus retest at the technical-inspection centre needs no fine and no legal change.
- [INFERENCE] Legal path: a Ringelmann-style grade from video is a screening number, not an opacimeter reading; in Hainan the fine is tied to a certified device and filing process, which Uzbekistan lacks. The payer is a government committee, so a pilot with a stated metric (precision at a threshold) and a human-in-the-loop evidence pack is realistic; fines are not.
- [CALC] USD 30-60 per camera-month x 100 cameras = USD 3-6k a month; 100 trucks a day at 5% smoky gives about 5 evidence packs per camera-day, which is plausible for a triage queue but needs the committee to own a field team.
- [INFERENCE] Tarp detection is an easier, parallel sub-claim (SAM 3 text prompt), but the decree text on the page does not mention covering trucks.

### Gaps
- Korea and Taiwan probes did not run; Chinese vendors, accuracy, and the Hainan device list unverified; the Pakistan report is a single unopened summary; no Uzbek rule for smoke opacity or who inspects roadside; the 2025 EPJ paper and CN108921147A not opened; whether smoky trucks are common enough on a filmable road near the venue is unknown.

## city-5 GateWash: construction-exit wheel-wash and tarp proof

### Takeaway
A new, specific regulatory hook exists: the Tashkent mayor's June 2026 draft says construction machinery must leave onto city streets clean, requires vehicle-washing areas, and routes site cameras into the Construction Control Inspectorate's system for about 1,700 sites. Prior art exists elsewhere (UK "Mud Cam" AI trial, a Hong Kong trial that checks vehicle cleaning and plates at worksite exits, Chinese smart-site vehicle-wash monitoring, Sensera gate monitoring with plate capture, and Delhi's regulator-run camera portal for construction sites) but none in CIS. Mud segmentation is the risky part; process verification is not. Verdict: sharpen to process-based proof and inspector integration.

### Cited Findings
#### Findings
- Tashkent draft rules (presented by the mayor, per Spot 10 Jun 2026): fenced sites with object passports; microclimate systems, water sprayers and dispensers on the perimeter; environmental sensors; "vehicle washing areas"; "Construction machinery must leave onto city streets in a clean state"; cameras whose feeds integrate into the Construction Control Inspectorate's system; nearly 1,700 sites would get a deadline; no penalties, dates or truck-cover rule on the page; still a draft under discussion — [Spot, 10 Jun 2026](https://www.spot.uz/ru/2026/06/10/construction-eco/) (re-checked)

### Inferences
- [CALC] 1,700 sites x USD 40 x 12 = about USD 816k a year at 100% penetration (one gate per site); 5-10% penetration = USD 41-82k. A draft rule without penalties gives weak willingness to pay now.

## city-6 LeafCheck: foliage-blocked signs and signals finder

### Takeaway
Technically easy and demoable, but it is a feature of road-asset platforms that already list sign and vegetation suites (Blyncsy "Signs", "Vegetation & Debris"; RoadAI "Overhanging vegetation"), the payer is a handful of district green-space departments at about USD 500 a year, and no Tashkent evidence of the problem turned up. Verdict: kill as a standalone company.

### Cited Findings
#### Findings
- Blyncsy lists product suites "Signs" and "Vegetation & Debris" and an "FHWA Compliance Alert: Automate Your Retroreflectivity Method Now" banner; its suites page says Signs covers detection, MUTCD inventory, condition assessment, retroreflectivity and OCR, and Vegetation & Debris covers "Vegetation encroachment detection" and debris, but neither is described as finding signs hidden by vegetation — [blyncsy.com](https://blyncsy.com/); [Blyncsy suites](https://blyncsy.com/suites/) (both re-checked)

### Inferences
- [CALC] About 200 district-level units x USD 500 a year = about USD 100k a year even at full adoption (district count is my rough figure, unsourced); a per-district licence cannot support a company.
- [INFERENCE] Known sign size gives distance (pixel size and EXIF focal length), so "visible area at 40/60/80 m" from one photo is computable; that part of the pitch is sound and the demo (judge photographs any sign behind a tree) is the easiest of the ten.
- [INFERENCE] The only durable variant is a broader sign-health audit (missing, damaged, faded, occluded) sold with other assets, which is another team's category (RoadAI, Blyncsy).
