## 1. Vehicle parc, new-car sales, UzAuto Motors/Chevrolet, BYD, Chinese EV/PHEV imports, used-car market, marketplaces, prices, vehicle-history services

### Takeaway
About 4.9-5.0M individually owned vehicles (93% passenger cars) and roughly 0.4M new vehicles sold a year, 77-83% of them Chevrolet built by UzAuto Motors and UzAuto Khorezm. Chinese brands, BYD and EV imports are surging while total new-car sales are shrinking in 2026. The used market runs at about 52-57k transactions a month. No Uzbek vehicle-history report product was found.

### Inferences
- Upper-bound inspection market [CALC]: ~625-690k used transactions a year (52-57k a month) x 300,000 UZS avtopodbor price (section 8) is roughly 187-205 bn UZS, about $16-17M, if every used deal bought a paid inspection; real capture would be a small fraction.
- Used deals (~1.5x new deals in Oct 2025 [CALC]) and low-trust intermediaries at markets make photo-based appraisal, mileage/paint checks and VIN-linked history the plainest gap; no incumbent history product was found.
- Customs EV imports (37.9k in H1 2026) are far above registered-fleet growth (about +22k in H1 2026 [CALC from 100k at end-2025 to 122.7k]); the gap is unexplained (registration lag, re-export or scope). Treat any "EV imports = EV fleet growth" assumption with care.
- Chinese brands (BYD, Chery, Haval, Kia-branded local assembly) now take about 18% of sales, so damage and parts models trained only on Chevrolet/Daewoo will miss a growing share.

## 2. EV adoption: fleet, incentives, charging stations, operators, connectors, reliability

### Takeaway
Registered EVs reached 122.7k on 1 Jul 2026 (+66.7% YoY), 72% of them in Tashkent city, driven by duty-free Chinese imports and cheap EV loans. Charging supply is thin (1,399 stations in May 2025 vs a 4,000 target for end-2026) and has been hit by two regulator-driven mass disconnections, so uptime, grid permits and ICE-blocking are real pain points.

## 3. CNG/methane and LPG

## 4. Insurance: OSAGO, KASKO, insurers, claims process, fraud, digital tools

### Takeaway
OSAGO is near-universal (6.2M contracts in force) and was repriced about 3.4-4x on 1 Jan 2026 with the limit doubled to 80M UZS; it is a small premium pool (406 bn UZS, about $34M) with a 60% payout ratio. Voluntary motor (KASKO-type) premiums halved in 2025 to 754 bn UZS. No Europrotocol-style no-police procedure, remote-inspection app or fraud statistics were found.

### Inferences
- OSAGO payout ratio 2025 = 245,224 / 406,165 = 60.4% [CALC]; KASKO-segment payout ratio = 107,758 / 754,268 = 14.3% [CALC]. A 14% loss ratio on a falling book suggests thin, bank-linked or low-claims voluntary cover, though the source gives no reason for the 47% fall.
- Average OSAGO premium written about 66,000 UZS per concluded contract (406,165 mln / 6,141,455 [CALC]) is above the 2025 tariff floor of 40,000-56,000, consistent with unlimited-driver and truck policies; 2026 prices are 3.4-4x higher, so premium volume should jump in 2026.
- KASKO contracts in force (660,550) are at most about 13% of the 4.98M private fleet [CALC, upper bound since the count includes non-car and corporate vehicles]. Property cover under OSAGO (28M UZS, about $2,400) is the realistic ceiling for photo-based claim triage; 15-day decision deadline is the SLA to beat.
- The main documented pain points are slow or refused claims (complaint share, 24% payout ratio in 2022) and premium leakage via kickbacks; there is no evidence of an organised photo-claims app.

### Gaps
- No Europrotocol-style no-police accident registration found for Uzbekistan (the retrieved OSAGO rule texts only cover notification within 3 working days); absence of evidence only.
- No known claims-fraud statistics or named fraud patterns beyond premium kickbacks; no remote or self-inspection app identified.
- No motor-only insurer ranking, average OSAGO/KASKO claim size, or claim counts by line (only all-lines claim counts).
- The regulator is NAPP in current sources; the task's "Insurance Market Development Agency" name could not be confirmed.

## 5. Road safety: casualties, causes, enforcement cameras, fines, motorcycles/couriers, child seats, tint, pre-trip checks

### Takeaway

### Inferences
- Pedestrians feature in about 46% of Jan-Sep 2025 crashes (2,689 / 5,895 [CALC]); fatal share in Q1 2026 was 31%, so pedestrian-priority detection and speed management remain the highest-yield safety targets.

## 6. Fleets: taxi and ride-hailing, delivery couriers, trucking and borders, public transport
