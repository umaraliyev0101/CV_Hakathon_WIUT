## wild-8 CamAudit: synthetic stress tests for road-camera AI claims

### Takeaway
This idea is better supported locally than its own pitch says. A Cabinet resolution of 25 Mar 2024 already writes the "exam" into Uzbek law (at least 95% plate capture day and night, use suspended when data errors reach 5%, a 10-day no-fine trial run by the competent body) but leaves "error", the counting base and the measuring party undefined, which is exactly the gap CamAudit fills. Weak points: a synthetic night/rain test cannot reproduce IR-illuminated ANPR hardware, generative edits can corrupt plate glyphs, per-frame confidence bounds are overconfident when variants share sources, the "one in five fines contested" hook is unsourced, and the idea names the wrong payers. Scores: novelty 4, feasibility 3, demo 4, business 3, moat 2, local fit 5. Verdict: keep, with the Resolution 148 re-anchoring below.

### Cited Findings

**The exam already exists on paper (local rules)**
- Cabinet of Ministers Resolution No. 148 of 25.03.2024, in force 26.03.2024, "Measures to improve the system for recording road traffic violations using automated photo and video fixation hardware and software" (paraphrased header read from the PDF page; the body of that PDF was not extracted) — [lex.uz PDF](https://lex.uz/pdfs/6847497)

**Arithmetic [CALC]**
- 15 to 40M UZS is $1,271 to $3,390, or 2.1 to 5.6 average monthly wages. Against the 2022 auction price of about 724M UZS per Tashkent camera location (66.61 bn / 92), an audit is about 2 to 5.5% of one location's price.
- Wilson 95% upper bounds: 0 errors in 100 frames 3.7%, in 300 frames 1.26%, in 1,000 frames 0.38%, in 3,000 frames 0.13%.
- Pseudo-replication: 1,000 synthetic variants drawn from S source clips have effective sample size n / (1 + (m - 1) rho) with m = 1000 / S variants per source. For S = 50 and within-source error correlation rho = 0.5 to 0.8 the design effect is 10.5 to 16.2 and n_eff is 62 to 95; an observed 5% error then has Wilson 95% roughly [1.7 to 2.3%, 11.7 to 13.3%] instead of [3.8%, 6.5%]. For S = 300 sources the gap is small (n_eff 350 to 460). DRIVE-C itself starts from 10 clips.
- FLUX throughput on an L4 (a guess scaled from BFL's RTX 5090 figure, not measured): 3.6 s per edit gives 1,000 edits in 1.0 h for $0.80; 8 s gives 2.2 h and $1.78, both at about 1 MP, not 4K.

### Inferences
- Right payer, not the idea's list: under Resolution 148 installation is paid from a state fund and every system must clear a ten-day no-fine trial and stay under 5% data errors, so the natural buyers are integrators and contractors supplying cameras (a pre-trial rehearsal that predicts which conditions will break the 95% capture line and the 5% error line), the Road Traffic Safety Service and the fund as a neutral measurer, and legacy private owners from the 2021 to 2022 auctions facing the 2025 inventory. Insurers have no role in the text I read. The state buyer is unlikely to pay a student startup, and vendors will not pay to be graded unless a rule forces it, so integrators with a trial to pass are the realistic first customers.
- The law sets numbers but not a method: "error", the 5% base, who counts and the 95% capture definition (day and night, per passing vehicle) are open. A transparent, reproducible measurement protocol is the product; the synthetic sliders are the demo.
- Do not sell "appeal survival". Appeals turn on evidence chain, notice, signage and calibration, not only on detection; the gov.uz page says nothing about errors. "Spec-compliance risk against the 95% and 5% lines" is checkable; "appeal risk" is not.
- Sim-to-real is the make-or-break: dedicated ANPR cameras use IR illumination, retroreflective plates and short shutters, so a day frame darkened in software does not reproduce a night capture. The defensible claim is a calibration curve: synthetic error versus error on about 100 real night plates, shown side by side. A 50-minute genuinely dark window exists on Day 1 before the 19:10 log-out [CALC]. Rain in Tashkent in October is unlikely to be available, so rain claims should rest on ACDC-style detector comparisons or be labelled synthetic-only.
- Generative edits must not touch plate pixels: the klein card warns that rendered text may be distorted, which would silently change the ground-truth characters. Use photometric transforms and `imagecorruptions` over the plate crop, and use FLUX only on the scene around it or on crops with the plate composited back unchanged. Edits at about 1 MP downsample the plate, so "plate reader fails" can be a resize artefact; run on crops.
- Report intervals that respect clustering (per vehicle or source clip). With 50 source clips the per-frame Wilson bounds the pitch promises are too tight by a factor of about 2 to 3 in width.
- Ground truth is a hidden cost: the 500-image Central Asia set may carry only country labels, so plate text must be hand-labelled (budget the time), and Qwen3-VL as "adjudicator of disagreements" is an unvalidated LLM judge on a metric the law treats as legal-grade; use human labels for the pass/fail numbers and keep the VLM as a triage helper.
- Sample-size arithmetic matters for the 95% line [CALC]: showing a miss or error rate below 5% with 95% confidence and zero failures needs about 60 (rule of three) to 73 (Wilson) independent vehicles, and below 1% needs about 300 to 380 (0 errors in 300 gives a Wilson upper bound of 1.26%); synthetic variants of the same vehicle do not count as independent.
- Moat stays low: the pipeline is open source and replicable in about a week; the durable assets are a labelled local night/rain plate set and being accepted as the measuring party, neither of which exists yet.
- Demo is the strongest asset: sliders on a Tashkent junction frame plus a "try your own plate photo" box work on any phone; precompute the grid of variants so the page does not depend on a cold GPU. The junction frame is too far for plate reading, so use a close-range plate tab for the OCR part.

### Gaps
- Resolution 148 was read through a model-assisted summary of Uzbek text; clause numbers, the 95% wording (capture of plates of at least 95% of passing vehicles versus Spot's "recognition at night") and the ">= 5% errors" clause need a native read of Annex 2 and Nizom cl. 9, 12, 23 to 26 before anyone pitches them.
- Whether the 2023 ownership-cancellation rule still stands, who counts the 5% errors, whether third-party measurements are admissible, and how many integrators or private owners exist: not found.
- No Uzbek appeal or wrongful-fine statistics; the Russian "one in five" figure is unsourced.
- ACDC licence, BDD100K dataset terms and the Uzbek share of the 500-image plate set are unverified; FLUX.2 klein latency on an L4 is unmeasured.
- Only 2 of the web searches for this idea returned (one EN extended, one RU extended); a Russian query on appeals and a vendor-benchmark query were refused.

## matrix-7 Plug Watch: EV fire-load and illegal-charging map for garages

### Takeaway
The headline novelty, spotting improvised charging cables from existing garage CCTV, fails a simple pixel budget (a 10 mm cable is about 1 to 2 pixels wide at 5 to 8 m on 1080p), the other parts (EV counting by plate colour, a fire-load score) are either trivial or unvalidated, a per-circuit current sensor or smart socket would detect charging more reliably than a camera, and I found no local fire-loss evidence or paying buyer. The local hooks are real (EV boom, a new car-park code, EV-specific plates) but two of the idea's stated facts are off: the green plate marks only the region-code window and only EVs, and the March 2026 shut-offs were about paperwork, not overload. Scores: novelty 3, feasibility 2, demo 3, business 2, moat 2, local fit 4. Verdict: kill (salvage the verified green-window EV classifier as a module).

### Inferences
- The cable claim is the weak joint. At 1 to 2 pixels wide, after H.264 compression, in dim garages with black cable on dark floor, thin-structure segmentation is unreliable even with SAM 3; recall would collapse exactly where fire risk matters. Close-range cameras (2 to 3 m, 4K) could see it, but that is a purpose-placed camera per charging zone, not "existing garage CCTV", and it erases the $6 per camera cost story. The toy-car demo hides this: toy cables fill far more of the frame than a real cord does.
- Night IR cameras drop colour, so the green-window classifier works only on daylight or white-light cameras and only for EVs; the verified rule excludes hybrids, so plug-in hybrids (many local BYDs are DM-i models) would look like petrol cars, and a green window is a few dozen pixels at garage range. The fallback to "model recognition" (make and model) is a separate classifier with no named dataset.
- The fire-load score has no ground truth. EV fires are reported as less frequent per vehicle than ICE fires (weak source), the severity argument is about suppression difficulty, and nothing local ties EV density or improvised cables to losses; the only locally documented risk is grid overload from unplanned charger connections, which is a metering problem.
- A current clamp or smart socket per bay or per circuit detects charging directly, cheaply and at night; I did not check hardware prices. CV adds value only where nothing is metered and the operator cannot act anyway.
- Payer is unproven: 5% of an average monthly wage per camera is heavy for a residential complex, property-insurance penetration is low [teammate], the new code defines where legal charging may happen but adds no monitoring duty, and I found no complaints from garage operators. Tailwinds exist (122.7k EVs, 90% underground parking planned in New Tashkent, chargers moving into underground levels), so demand may appear in 2 to 3 years.
- Pitch risk: framing EVs as a fire hazard in front of a BYD-sponsored panel, with fire statistics that do not favour the claim, invites easy rebuttals.
- Salvage: the green-window classifier (and a charging-bay "ICE-ing" detector) as a module inside a charging-bay compliance product; or a guard-patrol phone app at 1 to 2 m that photographs charging bays, which is a checklist app, not a camera network.

## wow-13 ShadeRent: shade-priced parking bays from one panorama

### Takeaway
A quirky idea with a defensible honest core (shade can be measured and predicted per bay) but the proposed pipeline, one phone panorama into monocular metric depth into ray-cast shade, is the least reliable way to get it: for afternoon sun a 15% height error moves a tree's shadow edge by more than a bay width, the panorama sees only one side of every occluder, and 20 lots with timed ground truth is impossible in about 8.5 on-campus hours. The payer is thin (a $20 survey, an unquantified 5 to 10% uplift, on-street tariffs that are five flat zones), though the airport parking concession is a concrete single customer. Adjacent prior art exists (ShadeMap, a Telegram bot, a Taiwanese shade-parking app, a 2025 research library) but I found nothing that does bay-level shade pricing from a panorama. Scores: novelty 4, feasibility 3, demo 3, business 2, moat 2, local fit 3. Verdict: sharpen (measured shade from timed photos, predict-then-verify demo, aim at the airport operator and canopy installers).

### Inferences
- Geometry is the soft spot: monocular metric depth outdoors at 20 to 50 m has no published accuracy in the sources I read, and shade edges are most sensitive in the afternoon heat peak the product sells (17:00 error shifts of 2.6 m exceed a 2.5 m bay width); a single viewpoint sees only the faces of trees and walls that face the camera, tree canopies are porous (dappled), and leaf-on state differs between October and July. A honest result is "probably shaded / probably sunny" bands, not exact bays.
- Measured shade beats simulated shade for a hackathon: timed photos from one fixed vantage give ground truth directly, per-bay shade-hours interpolate through the sun path, and "predict 16:30 from earlier shots, overlay the real 16:30 photo" is a verification judges can watch. This is the sibling variant "Soya" in the idea text. It also meets the "prove it works" brief better than 20 lots.
- Business is weaker than it sounds. On-street Tashkent prices are five flat zones fixed by the khokim and compliance is poor, so per-bay premiums are neither enforceable nor priced; shopping malls usually park free; the one concrete customer is the airport concessionaire, who owes a reservation app and EV charging (shade tiers and charger siting would plug into both) but pays a 5% revenue share and is one buyer; evidence that shade lifts revenue is missing (one source suggests location dominates). A better wedge is where to build canopies or solar carports and where to put chargers, sold to lot owners and installers.
- "Night, rain, local plates" constraint: the product is daylight-only by design; say so up front and show haze or overcast handling rather than leaving the slot blank.
- Competition watch: shade-parking hobby apps exist and ShadeMap already offers an API; the moat is only per-lot measured shade history plus integrations, which are thin.
