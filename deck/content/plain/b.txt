@ Sotiladi Scout
tag: Finds cars for sale that are never posted online, from "for sale" notes in windows.
story: Many sellers just put a "Sotiladi" (for sale) note with a phone number behind the windshield. A dealer walks a market or street with a phone; the app reads each note, identifies the car and ranks a call list by expected profit.
why: These cars never appear on websites, so dealers who find them first get better deals. Today someone walks around writing numbers by hand.
uses: A dealer finds ten unlisted cars in one market walk || A trade-in buyer fills stock faster || A marketplace invites offline sellers online
buyers: Used-car dealers and car flippers.

@ Ko'pik
tag: Proves a gas-equipment leak test was really done, from a video of soapy joints.
story: After fitting methane or propane equipment, the technician brushes soapy water on every joint and films it. Ko'pik checks every joint was covered, spots bubbles that grow (a leak) and saves a dated record tied to the gas cylinder.
why: Gas leaks in cars can cause fires and explosions. The rules track cylinders but say nothing about proving the leak test, and a third of used-car listings run on gas.
uses: A conversion workshop proves every car passed a leak test || An inspection centre reviews tests remotely || An insurer checks a car's gas safety record
buyers: Gas-conversion workshops and inspection centres.

@ BallonID
tag: Reads a gas cylinder's stamped label and says if it is too old or untested.
story: Every gas cylinder has numbers stamped into the metal: serial, dates, pressure and gas type. They are hard to read under rust and paint. BallonID takes two photos lit from the side, reads the stamp and applies the official rules: refill, retest or refuse.
why: An out-of-date or wrong cylinder can explode; in Fergana a propane cylinder filled with methane did. Today checks look up the car's plate, not the cylinder itself.
uses: A filling station checks the cylinder before refuelling || An inspector reads stamps in seconds || A car buyer checks a gas-converted car
buyers: Inspection centres, car inspectors and gas stations.

@ GapGauge
tag: Measures the gaps between a car's body panels with a bank card as the ruler.
story: You hold any bank card next to the gap between, say, the door and the fender, and take photos. Because every bank card is exactly the same size, GapGauge measures the gap in millimetres and compares the left side of the car with the right.
why: After a crash and repair, panels rarely line up perfectly, so uneven gaps reveal hidden accidents. Inspectors check this by eye today.
uses: A buyer checks for hidden crash repairs || An inspector backs up a verdict with numbers || A trade-in desk prices repaired cars correctly
buyers: Car inspection firms and dealers' trade-in desks.

@ BoltSeal
tag: Tells whether a car panel was ever removed, from close-up photos of its bolts.
story: At the factory, the bolts holding doors, hood and fenders are painted over. If a panel was removed for repair, a wrench leaves marks and cracks the paint. BoltSeal checks close-up photos of each bolt and flags the ones that were turned.
why: It is a well-known trick of professional inspectors, but it needs expertise. Automating it gives any buyer or bank a quick hidden-repair check.
uses: A bank checks a car before lending on it || A trade-in desk spots a replaced fender || A buyer checks a car before a full inspection
buyers: Dealers' trade-in desks, auto lenders and inspectors.

@ Proof-of-Inspection
tag: Checks the inspector's work, not the car, so banks can trust inspection reports.
story: When a car inspector films an inspection, this app checks that all required steps were done: VIN read, odometer shown, paint gauge used. It also catches reused videos and VINs that don't match the documents.
why: Banks and marketplaces rely on inspection PDFs that nobody verifies. A lazy or dishonest inspector can approve a bad car.
uses: A bank accepts only verified inspections for car loans || A marketplace labels trustworthy inspection reports || An inspection firm proves its quality
buyers: Banks, leasing firms and car marketplaces.

@ Lowside
tag: Shows whether a used moped or motorcycle has been crashed, from photos.
story: When a bike falls, it scrapes the same places: levers, foot pegs, mirrors, engine covers. Lowside finds these points in photos, tells crash scrapes from normal wear, and draws a simple damage map.
why: Courier fleets buy and rent out many used mopeds, and hidden crash damage makes them unsafe and costly. There were 563 moped and scooter crashes in nine months of 2026.
uses: A courier fleet checks mopeds at hand-over and return || A lessor documents damage fairly || A buyer avoids a crashed bike
buyers: Moped leasing firms and courier fleets.

@ LemonBack
tag: A money-back guarantee for used cars, priced from a 10-minute photo check.
story: The seller records a guided 10-minute video of the car. Checks for hidden repairs, mileage and damage price the risk. If the buyer later finds a problem the video should have shown, an insurer buys the car back within 30 days.
why: Buyers fear hidden problems, which pushes prices down for honest sellers. A guarantee builds trust, and insurers need new products.
uses: An honest seller sells faster with a guarantee || A buyer gets their money back if the car hid a repair || An insurer gains a new product line
buyers: Private sellers pay; an insurer carries the risk.

@ Drip-ID
tag: Tells which fluid a car is leaking, using a printed card and two photos.
story: Put the card under the car's engine and take two photos ten minutes apart. Water from the air-conditioner evaporates; oil, gearbox fluid and coolant stay and have typical colours. Drip-ID names the fluid and how serious it is.
why: A leak can mean a cheap fix or a big repair. Most buyers cannot tell, and standard inspections often skip it.
uses: A buyer checks a used car for leaks || An inspector adds a leak test to the report || A driver knows whether to visit a garage
buyers: Car inspectors and used-car buyers.

@ TremorLab

why: Worn engine mounts cause vibration and damage, but mechanics judge them by feel. A measured number makes the diagnosis clear.
uses: A garage shows a customer why the mounts need replacing || A taxi park checks engines regularly || A buyer checks a used car's engine

@ FakeStop

why: Fake parts are common at bazaars and can fail dangerously, like brakes. A third of Russian repair shops met fakes in 2025.
uses: A mechanic checks parts before fitting them || A brand owner maps where fakes are sold || A buyer checks a box at the bazaar
